# 02 — Managerial, behavioral, HR rounds and salary negotiation

> Many good engineers clear technical rounds and lose the offer (or 3–5 LPA) here. Prepare written answers, then practise speaking them.

## STAR format
**S**ituation (1–2 lines) → **T**ask (your responsibility) → **A**ction (what *you* did — 60% of the answer) → **R**esult (numbers) → *Learning* (1 line).
Answer length: 1.5–2 minutes. Use "I" for your work, "we" for the team.

## Prepare 8 stories (write each in `my-stories.md`)
| # | Story | Covers questions like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AngularJS → React migration (see [migration notes](../angularjs-to-react-migration/README.md)) | biggest achievement, complex project, architecture decision |
| 2 | A production bug you debugged under pressure | handling pressure, debugging approach, ownership |
| 3 | A performance improvement | technical depth, measuring impact |
| 4 | A disagreement with a senior/architect/PO | conflict, communication, influence |
| 5 | A deadline you nearly missed / scope cut | prioritisation, trade-offs, stakeholder management |
| 6 | Helping/mentoring a teammate or onboarding someone | teamwork, leadership |
| 7 | A mistake you made | failure, learning, honesty |
| 8 | Learning something new fast (e.g. new library, TypeScript) | learning agility |

## Common questions and how to answer them

**"Tell me about yourself."** (90 seconds — the most important answer of the whole loop)
> I'm a frontend engineer with 4.5 years at Bosch. I've worked on a large enterprise application that started on AngularJS. Together with a senior engineer I designed and drove its incremental migration to React and TypeScript — understanding the legacy features, building a bridge layer so both frameworks could run side by side, and migrating screens one by one while we kept shipping new features in React. Along the way I worked a lot on component architecture, performance and testing — for example __ (one number). I'm now looking for a role where I can work on larger-scale products and grow toward a senior/lead frontend role — which is why this position at __ interests me.

**"Why are you leaving Bosch?"** — never criticise the employer.
> I've learned a lot in 4.5 years and the migration was a great experience. But I've been on the same product the whole time, and I want exposure to a larger scale / different domain / a modern stack end to end / bigger engineering teams. __'s work on __ is exactly that.

**"Why our company?"** — 2 specific reasons: their product/domain (research 10 min: e.g. Siemens Xcelerator / industrial software, Honeywell Forge / building automation, Qualcomm developer tools) + engineering culture/scale + how your experience fits.

**"Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?"** — senior → tech lead/architect in frontend, owning architecture and mentoring; deeper in the company's domain.

**"What are your strengths / weaknesses?"** Strength: understanding legacy systems and modernising them safely. Weakness: something real + what you're doing about it ("I used to go deep into implementation before aligning on scope; now I write a short design note first").

**"How do you handle disagreement?"** — data, prototypes, listen first, disagree-and-commit.

**"How do you ensure code quality?"** — TypeScript strict, lint rules, code reviews, tests (RTL), small PRs, CI checks, design docs for big changes.

**"Describe your team & process."** — team size, Scrum/sprints, your role in planning, code review, release process, how you work with backend/QA/UX.

**Questions to ask them (always ask 2):** What does success look like in the first 6 months? What's the frontend stack and biggest technical challenge right now? How is the team structured? How do code reviews and releases work? Is there legacy migration work (good for you!)?

## HR round
- Current CTC: be truthful (they may ask for payslips). Break-up: fixed + variable + benefits.
- Expected CTC: give a **range anchored at the top**: "Based on my experience and the market for this role I'm looking at 25–27 LPA fixed." Then let them respond.
- Notice period: state facts; mention buy-out/early release possibility if real.
- Other offers: if you have them, say so (with numbers if comfortable). It's the strongest lever you have.
- Relocation, work mode (hybrid/WFO), joining date.
- Background verification: dates and titles on your resume must match records exactly.

## Salary negotiation playbook
1. **Get multiple processes running at the same time** (aim for 2–3 offers in the same 2–3 weeks). This matters more than any script.
2. Don't share your expected number first if you can avoid it: "I'd like to understand the role and band first. What's the budgeted range?"
3. When you get an offer: thank them, ask for the **written break-up**, say you'll respond in 1–2 days. Never accept on the call.
4. Compare **fixed** pay (variable pay is often not paid out in full). Also joining bonus, ESOPs/RSUs (vesting), insurance, leave, WFH, learning budget.
5. Counter with reasons: "Thank you — I'm excited about the role. Considering my migration/architecture experience and another offer at __ LPA fixed, could you bring the fixed component to __?" Ask for ~10–15% above what you actually want.
6. If they can't move on fixed, ask for joining bonus, notice buy-out, earlier review, or a higher level/title.
7. Use the best offer to negotiate the one you actually want — honestly.
8. Once you accept, stop shopping. Resign professionally, keep a good relationship with Bosch.
